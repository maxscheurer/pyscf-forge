Derivation of _B_dot_x(δD)

The GOSTSHYP linear response kernel: element-wise derivative of the Fock operator with respect to the density matrix, contracted with a perturbation.

1. The GOSTSHYP Fock matrix element

Each element of the GOSTSHYP Fock contribution is:

$$ V_{\mu\nu}(D) = \sum_g \left[ \alpha_g(D) \, \tilde{G}_{g,\mu\nu} + \beta_g(D) \, \hat{F}_{g,\mu\nu} \right] $$

where:

$$ \alpha_g(D) = \frac{P \, A_g}{F_g(D)}, \qquad \beta_g(D) = -\frac{P \, A_g \, e_g(D)}{F_g(D)^2} $$

and the $D$-dependent scalars are:

$$ e_g(D) = \sum_{kl} D_{kl} \, \tilde{G}_{g,kl}, \qquad F_g(D) = \sum_{kl} D_{kl} \, \hat{F}_{g,kl} $$
What depends on $D$? Only $e_g$ and $F_g$ (and through them, $\alpha_g$ and $\beta_g$). The integral matrices $\tilde{G}_{g,\mu\nu}$ and $\hat{F}_{g,\mu\nu}$ are geometry-dependent only.

2. Element-wise derivative $\partial V_{\mu\nu} / \partial D_{\eta\gamma}$

We need:

$$ \frac{\partial V_{\mu\nu}}{\partial D_{\eta\gamma}} = \sum_g \left[ \frac{\partial \alpha_g}{\partial D_{\eta\gamma}} \, \tilde{G}_{g,\mu\nu} + \frac{\partial \beta_g}{\partial D_{\eta\gamma}} \, \hat{F}_{g,\mu\nu} \right] $$

2a. Derivatives of the scalars w.r.t. $D_{\kappa\lambda}$

$$ \frac{\partial e_g}{\partial D_{\kappa\lambda}} = \tilde{G}_{g,\kappa\lambda} $$ $$ \frac{\partial F_g}{\partial D_{\kappa\lambda}} = \hat{F}_{g,\kappa\lambda} $$

These follow directly from the definitions: $e_g = \sum_{kl} D_{kl} \tilde{G}_{g,kl}$ is linear in $D$.

2b. Derivative of $\alpha_g$

$$ \frac{\partial \alpha_g}{\partial D_{\kappa\lambda}} = P \, A_g \, \frac{\partial}{\partial D_{\kappa\lambda}} \left( F_g^{-1} \right) = P \, A_g \, \left( -F_g^{-2} \right) \, \frac{\partial F_g}{\partial D_{\kappa\lambda}} $$
$$ \frac{\partial \alpha_g}{\partial D_{\kappa\lambda}} = -\frac{P \, A_g}{F_g^2} \, \hat{F}_{g,\kappa\lambda} $$

2c. Derivative of $\beta_g$

$$ \beta_g = -P \, A_g \, e_g \, F_g^{-2} $$

Product rule on $e_g \cdot F_g^{-2}$:

$$ \frac{\partial \beta_g}{\partial D_{\kappa\lambda}} = -P \, A_g \left[ \frac{\partial e_g}{\partial D_{\kappa\lambda}} \cdot F_g^{-2} + e_g \cdot \left(-2 F_g^{-3}\right) \frac{\partial F_g}{\partial D_{\kappa\lambda}} \right] $$
$$ \frac{\partial \beta_g}{\partial D_{\kappa\lambda}} = -\frac{P \, A_g}{F_g^2} \, \tilde{G}_{g,\kappa\lambda} + \frac{2 \, P \, A_g \, e_g}{F_g^3} \, \hat{F}_{g,\kappa\lambda} $$

3. The full 4-index object

Substituting back:

$$ \frac{\partial V_{\mu\nu}}{\partial D_{\kappa\lambda}} = \sum_g \left[ -\frac{P A_g}{F_g^2} \, \hat{F}_{g,\kappa\lambda} \, \tilde{G}_{g,\mu\nu} + \left( -\frac{P A_g}{F_g^2} \, \tilde{G}_{g,\kappa\lambda} + \frac{2 P A_g e_g}{F_g^3} \, \hat{F}_{g,\kappa\lambda} \right) \hat{F}_{g,\mu\nu} \right] $$

Rearranging by which integral matrix carries the $(\kappa\lambda)$ index:

$$ \frac{\partial V_{\mu\nu}}{\partial D_{\kappa\lambda}} = \sum_g P A_g \left[ -\frac{1}{F_g^2} \, \hat{F}_{g,\kappa\lambda} \, \tilde{G}_{g,\mu\nu} -\frac{1}{F_g^2} \, \tilde{G}_{g,\kappa\lambda} \, \hat{F}_{g,\mu\nu} + \frac{2 e_g}{F_g^3} \, \hat{F}_{g,\kappa\lambda} \, \hat{F}_{g,\mu\nu} \right] $$
Structure: This is a rank-2 tensor in $(\mu\nu)$ and a rank-2 tensor in $(\kappa\lambda)$, summed over grid points $g$. Each term is a product of an integral matrix at $(\mu\nu)$ with an integral matrix at $(\kappa\lambda)$.

4. Contraction with the perturbation: _B_dot_x(δD)

The linear response is:

$$ \texttt{B\_dot\_x}(\delta D)_{\mu\nu} = \sum_{\kappa\lambda} \frac{\partial V_{\mu\nu}}{\partial D_{\kappa\lambda}} \, \delta D_{\kappa\lambda} $$

Defining the perturbed traces (contracting over $\kappa\lambda$):

$$ \delta e_g \equiv \sum_{\kappa\lambda} \delta D_{\kappa\lambda} \, \tilde{G}_{g,\kappa\lambda} = \mathrm{Tr}[\delta D \cdot \tilde{G}_g] $$ $$ \delta F_g \equiv \sum_{\kappa\lambda} \delta D_{\kappa\lambda} \, \hat{F}_{g,\kappa\lambda} = \mathrm{Tr}[\delta D \cdot \hat{F}_g] $$

we get:

$$ \boxed{ \texttt{B\_dot\_x}(\delta D)_{\mu\nu} = \sum_g P A_g \left[ -\frac{\delta F_g}{F_g^2} \, \tilde{G}_{g,\mu\nu} -\frac{\delta e_g}{F_g^2} \, \hat{F}_{g,\mu\nu} + \frac{2 \, e_g \, \delta F_g}{F_g^3} \, \hat{F}_{g,\mu\nu} \right] } $$

Or equivalently, defining per-grid-point amplitude perturbations:

$$ \delta\alpha_g = -\frac{P A_g}{F_g^2} \, \delta F_g $$ $$ \delta\beta_g = -\frac{P A_g}{F_g^2} \, \delta e_g + \frac{2 P A_g \, e_g}{F_g^3} \, \delta F_g $$ $$ \texttt{B\_dot\_x}(\delta D)_{\mu\nu} = \sum_g \left[ \delta\alpha_g \, \tilde{G}_{g,\mu\nu} + \delta\beta_g \, \hat{F}_{g,\mu\nu} \right] $$

5. Verification

5a. Consistency with analytical_grad_vmat

In the gradient code, the "coefficient response" computes $\partial\alpha_g/\partial R$ and $\partial\beta_g/\partial R$ at fixed $D$. Those are:

$$ \frac{\partial\alpha_g}{\partial R} = P \left[\frac{\partial A_g/\partial R}{F_g} - \frac{A_g \, \partial F_g/\partial R}{F_g^2}\right] $$ $$ \frac{\partial\beta_g}{\partial R} = -P \left[\frac{(\partial A_g/\partial R) \, e_g}{F_g^2} + \frac{A_g \, (\partial e_g/\partial R)}{F_g^2} - \frac{2 A_g \, e_g \, (\partial F_g/\partial R)}{F_g^3}\right] $$

Our $\delta\beta_g$ matches the last two terms (with $\partial e_g/\partial R \to \delta e_g$, $\partial F_g/\partial R \to \delta F_g$), and correctly has no $\partial A_g$ terms because $A_g$ does not depend on $D$. ✓

5b. Finite-difference test

$$ \texttt{B\_dot\_x}(\delta D) \approx \frac{V(D + \varepsilon\,\delta D) - V(D - \varepsilon\,\delta D)}{2\varepsilon} $$

5c. Symmetry

Since $\tilde{G}_g$ and $\hat{F}_g$ are symmetric in $(\mu\nu)$, the result is symmetric. ✓

5d. The response is symmetric in the perturbation indices

Is $\partial V_{\mu\nu}/\partial D_{\kappa\lambda} = \partial V_{\mu\nu}/\partial D_{\lambda\kappa}$?

Yes, because $\tilde{G}_{g,\kappa\lambda} = \tilde{G}_{g,\lambda\kappa}$ and $\hat{F}_{g,\kappa\lambda} = \hat{F}_{g,\lambda\kappa}$ (the integral matrices are symmetric). So the response only depends on the symmetric part of $\delta D$. ✓
